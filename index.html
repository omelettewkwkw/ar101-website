The University of Manila

History

The University of Manila began on October 5, 1913, when five young people, among them the siblings Apolinario, Mariano and Maria de los Santos, met over breakfast in Intramuros and resolved to build a school for Filipino youth. They called it Instituto de Manila, with Apolinario de los Santos as its first director.

Classes started in school year 1914-1915 with elementary and high school levels in Binondo. By 1919-1920 the school had its own building in Sampaloc, where the campus still stands. On November 3, 1921 it was re-incorporated as the University of Manila.

New colleges followed over the years, beginning with the College of Law in 1919, and its graduates became known for their strong results in the Bar Examinations. The university grew under the leadership of the de los Santos family, and later under Dr. Emily Dodson De Leon, who became president in 2006. In 1994 it opened four-year programs including Computer Science, and in 2003 it became the first university in the country recognized to offer a degree in the Japanese language.

Mission

The university's mission is summed up in the word ACE:

  • A – Anchor in the youth a love of country, science and virtue.
  • C – Create a community of academic excellence and competence.
  • E – Empower young people with modern scientific and technological skills so they can face the challenges of a global society.

Vision

To pass on to future generations the university's three ideals, love of country, love of science and love of virtue, so that life becomes better, brighter and easier for others, and quality education is within reach of everyone.

Motto

Patria, Scientia, et Virtus (Country, Science and Virtue).

The university also encourages students to gain knowledge and skills that benefit all of humanity.

Computer Science

History

The College of Computer Science and Information Technology offers a Bachelor of Science in Computer Science and an Associate in Computer Technology. The university opened its four-year Computer Science program on February 16, 1994.

Faculty members carry out research in areas such as computer vision, programming languages, machine learning, databases, modeling and simulation, and the theory of computation. Students can take on independent study and work with faculty on research projects. The college is led by Dr. Richard D. de Leon, Dean of the College of Computer Science and Engineering.

Mission

To give students a strong undergraduate and graduate education in both the theory and practice of computer science, and to train them to solve real-world problems, so they are ready for long and rewarding careers in a fast-changing global workplace.

Vision

To build a strong research and teaching environment that responds quickly to the challenges of the 21st century.
